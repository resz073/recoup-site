Recoup privacy policy

Effective 1 November 2026

Recoup is an Android app for keeping track of money you spend for your office until it's paid back, and money you lend or borrow. This policy explains what Recoup for Android does with your information.

In short: your ledger and receipt photos stay on your phone, encrypted with your PIN. If you choose to sign in with Google, encrypted copies are also kept in your own Google Drive. Nobody else receives your data, including the developer.

Your ledger

Everything you enter, such as people's names, amounts, dates, notes, purchases and bills, is stored on your phone in an encrypted file. It's encrypted with AES-256 using a key that's protected by your PIN. Your recovery key, and your fingerprint if you turn fingerprint unlock on, can also open it. The developer has no copy of your data and no way to read it.

Receipt photos

You can add photos of receipts to a purchase, by taking one with your phone's camera app or choosing one in Android's photo picker. Recoup receives only the picture you take or choose; it doesn't ask for permission to use the camera or to see your photos. Each picture is resized and saved again, which also removes details the camera adds, such as where the photo was taken. It's then encrypted on your phone with its own key, which is kept inside your encrypted ledger. A photo from the camera stays in Recoup's private storage only until it's encrypted, and is then deleted.

Reminders and the home-screen widget

Recoup can remind you the day before a loan is due, and when a bill has waited 30 days in billing (then every week). You can turn each kind off in the Account tab, or for one loan on its page. While your phone is locked (with a PIN, pattern, password or fingerprint), a reminder says only something general, such as "A loan is due soon"; the name and amount show once the phone is unlocked.

The widget shows your net position and totals. Its amounts are hidden unless you turn on Show amounts on widget, because anyone who sees your home screen could read them.

So that reminders and the widget work while Recoup is locked, Recoup keeps a small summary on your phone: the totals, and the names, amounts and dates of loans with a due date and of bills in billing. It's encrypted with a key kept in your phone's secure hardware (the Android Keystore), so it can't be read off the phone. It never leaves your phone, and Erase data deletes it.

Signing in with Google (optional)

You can use Recoup without an account. If you choose to sign in with Google, Recoup asks Google for two things:

  • Your name and email address, only to show which Google account is in use in Recoup. They're kept on your phone.
  • Access to Recoup's own hidden folder in your Google Drive (the app data folder, permission drive.appdata). Recoup uses it to keep an encrypted copy of your ledger, so it can be synced between your phones and brought back on a new phone; an encrypted copy from the start of each day for the last 14 days, so you can restore an earlier version; and encrypted copies of your receipt photos, so they come back on a new phone too. Recoup can't see, open or change any other file in your Google Drive.

The copies are encrypted on your phone before they're uploaded, so neither Google nor anyone else can read them without your PIN or recovery key. Recoup talks to Google directly from your phone; there is no Recoup server.

Signing in doesn't create a Recoup account. There are no Recoup accounts: signing in only lets Recoup use its hidden folder in your own Google Drive.

Recoup's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

What Recoup doesn't do

  • It doesn't send your data to the developer or to anyone else.
  • It has no ads, analytics or tracking, and it doesn't sell or share any data.
  • It doesn't use your Google account data for anything except showing the signed-in account and the sync and restore described above.

Files you save

When you choose Download backup, Recoup saves an encrypted backup file wherever you pick (it holds your ledger, not receipt photos). When you choose Download spreadsheet, it asks for your PIN and then saves a .csv file; that file isn't encrypted, so keep it somewhere safe. Recoup reads a file only when you pick one in Add from a backup file.

Permissions

  • Internet: only to reach Google Drive when you're signed in.
  • Notifications (optional, asked for on Android 13 and later): only to show reminders.
  • Run at startup: so reminders are still on time after the phone restarts.
  • Fingerprint (optional): handled by Android. Recoup never sees your fingerprint.

Android also gives Recoup a few permissions without asking, used by its reminder scheduling: running a short background job, keeping the phone awake while it runs, and checking whether the phone is online.

Recoup doesn't ask for access to your camera, photos, contacts or location.

Deleting your data

There's no Recoup account to delete: your data is only on your phone and, if you signed in with Google, in your own Google Drive. Here's how to remove it.

  • Erase data, in Recoup's Account tab, deletes your ledger, receipt photos, PIN and settings from the phone, and stops its reminders.
  • Sign out, in the same tab, stops syncing and takes back Recoup's access to your Google Drive.
  • To delete the copies in Google Drive, including receipt photos, open Google Drive on the web, go to Settings, then Manage apps, find Recoup, and choose Options, then Delete hidden app data. You can also remove Recoup's access to your Google account at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Children

Recoup is meant for adults keeping track of their own money. It isn't directed at children.
